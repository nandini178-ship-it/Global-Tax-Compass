Step 6 — DTAA Article 4 Tie-Breaker Test
Resolving Dual / Multiple Residency
You appear to be a tax resident of simultaneously. The following DTAA Article 4 sequential tests determine your final country of residence for treaty purposes.
a
Permanent Home Available
In which country do you have a permanent home available to you — a place you own or rent long-term, available to you at all times year-round? If in both countries, this test is not conclusive.
A hotel or short-term rental does not count as a permanent home. If only one country → this is your final residence. If both or neither → proceed to (b).
b
Centre of Vital Interests
In which country are your closest personal and economic relations? Consider: where your family lives, where your main business/employment is, where your assets are held, where your social and cultural connections are strongest.
Used when permanent home test is inconclusive. If determinable → conclusive. If not → proceed to (c).
c
Habitual Abode
In which country do you have your habitual abode — the place where you most regularly and consistently stay, the place that is your day-to-day base of life?
Not just the most days this year — but where you habitually stay as a matter of routine. If determinable → conclusive. If not → proceed to (d).
d
Nationality
Of which country are you a national / citizen?
Final tiebreaker. If national of both or neither, the matter is resolved by mutual agreement (MAP) between the two tax authorities under DTAA Article 25.

Why We Do It

Our story

Global Tax Compass was built by a team of tax professionals with more than 10 years of international consulting experience. Over a decade of advising individuals and businesses across borders, we kept seeing the same problem: people splitting their time between countries had no single, reliable way to work out where they actually stood on tax residency.

That gap is what led us to build this platform. After carefully studying how residency tests, DTAA tie-breaker rules, and filing obligations vary from one jurisdiction to the next, we set out to create a common platform where anyone can enter their travel and work details and get a clear read on their residency status and tax exposure — no matter how many countries they've lived and worked in.
